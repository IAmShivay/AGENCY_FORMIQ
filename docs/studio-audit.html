The site looks good. What it sells is wrong for Europe and Dubai.

The new monochrome studio layer (typography, motion, wordmark) is a good base. The problem is underneath it. The words, the numbers and the offer all describe a Rs 6,999/month package for local shops in West Bengal. A buyer in Berlin or Dubai reads it as cheap, templated and unverifiable. That reaction is what "AI-generated" really means here, and the design is not the cause.

1. Fix this week

Do these before you send any traffic from Europe. Some of them are risks on their own, separate from the positioning work.

Critical · securityAny signed-up user can make themselves admin

app/api/admin/setup/route.ts sets is_admin: true, role: 'admin' for whoever is logged in, and components/login.tsx:174 allows public sign-up. Supabase row-level security (RLS) may still block the update, so check your users table policies. Either way the route should not exist in production. Delete it, or guard it with a server-side secret or an allow-listed email. The auth redirect in middleware.ts:69-73 is also commented out, so /admin is protected only on the client.

Critical · EU lawTracking fires with no cookie consent

The Meta Pixel and the Google Ads gtag load on every page in app/layout.tsx:113-145, before any consent. There is no consent banner anywhere. Under GDPR/ePrivacy that is not allowed for EU and UK visitors, and European prospects notice it. Add a consent manager (CookieYes, Cookiebot, or a small custom one with Google Consent Mode v2) and load the pixels only after the visitor opts in.

High · trustThe privacy policy and terms won't pass a European buyer's check

The privacy page says "Last updated January 2024". It does not name a data controller or mention GDPR, UK GDPR or the UAE PDPL, and it doesn't name your processors (Meta, Google, Supabase, Brevo). The terms point only to Indian courts. Rewrite both, name the legal entity, and list your processors. EU clients will also ask you for a DPA (data processing agreement), so prepare a template.

High · credibilityAdSense ads on your own blog

There are five ad slots in app/(routes)/blog. A studio that shows Google ads beside its own articles looks like a content farm. Remove them.

Medium · brokenThings a prospect or Google will run into
  • app/sitemap.ts:196-221 lists /portfolio/*/{id} pages that don't exist, so they return 404.
  • The portfolio fallback images in /images/portfolio/*.jpg point to a folder that doesn't exist.
  • The microphone error in AIBookingAgents.tsx:195 tells visitors to "Open via http://localhost:3001".
  • /theme-showcase, /landing and /services/checkout (a hardcoded $99.99 cart) are all public.
  • The nav says "AI & Ml", and the team page writes "Swagata mahtree" in lowercase.
  • The footer links to llms.txt, Sitemap and Robots. Those are for crawlers, not visitors.
  • The Google site verification is still the placeholder 'your-google-verification-code'.

2. Why it reads as AI-generated

Visitors decide a site is "AI-made" mostly from the copy and the evidence, and only partly from the layout. These are the specific tells on your site:

Numbers that contradict each other

ClaimWhere
50+ clientsHero, TrustBar, Testimonials, FinalCTA (about 8 places)
25+ clients servedAbout page
500+ projects deliveredPortfolioShowcase, BenefitsSection
500+ MVPs built/landing
5M+ user downloads, 12 industry awards/portfolio/applications
5 industry awards, 99% satisfaction/portfolio/websites
98% satisfaction, 24/7 supportPortfolioShowcase, About

One careful reader who adds these up will stop trusting everything else on the site. Keep only numbers you can prove, and state each one in a single place.

People nobody can verify

  • The hero shows an avatar stack of letters "R / A / S / +47" with no faces.
  • All six testimonials use same-colored initials with no photos, no links and generic business names. They sit under a "Verified Google Reviews" label without linking to any review.
  • The team page uses the same Freepik stock avatar for all four people, and every social link is #.
  • Two of the testimonials in SocialProof.tsx are credited to "Shivay", the founder.
  • The hosting page has "David Wilson / Jennifer Lee" with randomuser.me portraits, and /portfolio/websites has "Sarah Johnson, TechVision Inc."

Stock images in place of your own work

Every homepage portfolio card uses an Unsplash photo. The "client meetings" image is from Pexels. The hero video file is named gemini_generated_video_…mp4. Two of the three showcased projects live on *.formiqstudio.in subdomains, so they look like your own demos. The third project's link is #.

Template rhythm

  • Everything comes in threes: three steps, three tiers, three projects, three agent cards, three blog cards, three hero bullets, three hero stats, three metrics per project.
  • The same card grid repeats section after section: a lucide icon, a heading and two lines.
  • The same decorations repeat: pill eyebrows with a pulsing dot, Sparkles icons, blurred gradient blobs, a "Most Popular" star pill, and random floating orbs (Math.random()).
  • Six service pages share one template (ServicePageLayout): four cards of four features, four steps, then "Ready to…?".

Copy that could belong to anyone

"Create something amazing together". "Cutting-edge technologies". "Supercharge your digital workflow". "Dominate marketplaces". "Guaranteed ROI". "Real Results from Real Businesses". "Everything you need to grow — all included". Headings are in Title Case. Em dashes are everywhere. None of these sentences would change if a different agency's name were swapped in.

Selling on discounts

The site uses "Starting at just Rs 6,999", "Rs 233/day · Best value", "Same team agencies charge Rs 25,000+ for", a checkmark table against agencies and freelancers, "30-day money-back · no questions asked" and "2 slots left this month" (fake scarcity). These convert for price-sensitive local buyers. For a European marketing lead or a Dubai founder they say budget vendor.

3. How to make it feel human-made

Most of this comes from evidence and specifics, not visual effects. A good rule: every section should contain something only you could have written.

Faces and names

Real photos of you and the team at your desks in Durgapur. Use one photographer and one light setup, not stock. Give each person a name, a role, a one-line opinion and a LinkedIn link. Show fewer people if that's what's real.

Your actual work, big

Full-width screenshots and screen recordings of the real sites, plus process artefacts: wireframes, rejected logo sketches, Figma boards. Annvaya packaging photographed on a table beats any Unsplash image.

Specific numbers, sourced

Not "3× lead growth". Write "LuxeHouse went from 11 to 26 enquiries a month in the 90 days after launch (GA4, Jan–Mar)". One true, specific number is worth ten round ones.

A voice with opinions

Write the way you talk to a client. Short sentences. Say what you don't do. Use sentence case. Cut most em dashes. Read it aloud: if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.

Fewer, odder sections

Break the three-card rhythm. Use one long case-study scroll, a single quote set large, a plain-text list of clients. Drop most icons; a studio's own work is the illustration.

Founder presence

A short note from you near the bottom of the homepage, with a real signature or photo, saying why the studio exists and who it's for. Link a 60-second Loom of you talking. Nothing makes a site feel human faster.

Design layer: keep, trim, remove

KeepTrimRemove
The monochrome base, Bricolage + Plex Mono, the wordmark, magnetic buttons, the marquee (once), Lenis scrolling Use the preloader only on the first visit and under 1.2s, with a studio line ("Formiq — independent design studio, est. 20XX") in place of a sales slogan. Use the route curtain only between top-level pages. The 16 neon/cyberpunk/matrix themes and their CSS (91 references in globals.css), gradient blobs, pulsing-dot pills, Sparkles icons, random orbs, outline-stroke headline text, the custom cursor on touch devices. Pick one accent color (Cobalt or Ember are the strongest) and drop the picker.

4. Positioning for Europe and Dubai

Don't sell your Indian offer in euros. Run two front doors. The international site is the studio. The local package (Rs 6,999) moves to its own page or subdomain, for example local.formiqstudio.in or /india, so it stops setting your price anchor.

Pick a sharp focus

"Websites, apps, ads, SEO, Shopify, AI, hosting, domains" is a list that makes a buyer pick the cheapest vendor. Lead with one or two things you can prove, for example:

  • Brand + website for D2C and food/wellness brands. Annvaya already proves this: packaging, identity, Shopify.
  • Web and product for B2B SaaS startups. ClearCRM proves this, if you can present it as client-style work.
  • Dubai angle: websites and booking flows for hospitality, clinics, real estate and F&B, with your AI booking agents as the differentiator. That market spends money and expects things fast.

Price like a studio

Show "Projects typically start from" in EUR, GBP and AED, not monthly subscriptions. These are realistic entry points for a small offshore studio with good work:

OfferEuropeDubai
Brand identityfrom €3,500from AED 14,000
Marketing website (Next.js / Framer / Webflow)€5,000–12,000AED 20,000–45,000
Shopify store€6,000–15,000AED 25,000–55,000
Monthly retainer (design + dev)from €1,800/mofrom AED 7,000/mo

Those prices are still 40–60% below a local agency in Amsterdam or Dubai. That is your advantage, but say it as "senior work, efficient team", not "cheap". Remove money-back guarantees, "slots left" and comparison tables.

Make it feel close

  • Timezone: IST is 1.5h ahead of Dubai and 3.5–4.5h ahead of CET/UK. Say so: "Our day overlaps fully with Dubai and covers European mornings and afternoons." Replace the footer's Durgapur clock with a London / Dubai / Durgapur trio.
  • Contact: email and booking first; WhatsApp is fine for Dubai (people there use it heavily), but drop it for Europe. Get a UAE virtual number and a UK number (Twilio, Aircall or similar).
  • Domain: a .com or .studio domain for the international site. Keep .in for the local business.
  • Payments and invoicing: invoice in EUR/GBP/AED through Stripe (the routes already exist) or Wise. File a GST LUT so services you export are zero-rated, and remove Cashfree from the international flow.
  • Contact form: name, company, website, budget band (in the visitor's currency), timeline. Drop the required +91 phone field.

6. Homepage blueprint

Seven sections in place of twelve. Put the work second, not sixth.

  1. Hero. One sentence that says who you are and who you help, plus one piece of real work in motion.
    Get more customers without the confusion.
    "Formiq is a small design and development studio. We build brands and websites for founders who care how things look and how they work."
    One CTA: "See the work". A secondary text link: "Book a call".
  2. Selected work. 3–4 projects, large and full width, each with a one-line outcome: "Annvaya — identity, 15 packaging SKUs, Shopify. Launched in 6 weeks."
  3. What we do. Three short paragraphs, no icons. What it includes, how long it takes, and who it's for.
  4. How we work. Your real process and timezone overlap, with a photo of the team or a Figma board.
  5. Words from clients. One or two quotes set large, with full name, role, company, photo and a link. Show fewer rather than any you can't verify.
  6. Founder note. About 80 words from you, with a photo and a signature.
  7. Contact. "Have a project in mind?" plus booking and email. Show starting prices here or on the Services page.

Update the metadata to match: title "Formiq — Design & development studio", OG locale en_GB, schema areaServed covering EU, UK and AE, and the right hreflang once the India page is split off.

7. How to win EU and Dubai clients

A better site converts the visitors you get, but it won't bring visitors from Europe or Dubai by itself. Most first international clients come from outbound work and platforms.

Get listed on agency platforms

Clutch, Sortlist (strong in Europe), DesignRush, GoodFirms. Ask 3–5 existing clients for reviews there. These platforms rank for "agency in [city]" searches you can't win on your own.

Post work where designers look

Behance and Dribbble case studies of Annvaya and LuxeHouse. Submit the new site to Awwwards/CSSDA once it's done. That's how studios get noticed abroad.

Send audits on video

Pick 20 brands a week (Shopify D2C in DE/NL/UK, clinics or restaurants in Dubai). Record a 2-minute Loom pointing out three concrete fixes on their site. Send it on LinkedIn or by email. Reply rates are many times higher than generic pitches.

White-label for local agencies

Dubai and European agencies are short of developers. Offer them overflow development and design under their brand. It's often the fastest route to steady euro or dirham revenue, and their clients become your case studies.

Founder on LinkedIn

Three posts a week: work in progress, before/after shots, short lessons. European and Dubai buyers check the founder before the website.

Dubai in person

Dubai is a few hours' flight from Kolkata. One trip with 10 pre-booked meetings, or a stand at a startup event (GITEX, Step), often pays for itself. Hospitality and real estate buyers there prefer meeting face to face.

What European buyers will check before signing

  • A registered company name, address and VAT/GST number in the footer and on invoices.
  • A GDPR-compliant site, plus a DPA and a contract template (scope, IP transfer on payment, 50/50 or 40/30/30 milestones).
  • Clear ownership: they own the code, the Figma files and the domain.
  • Reachability during their working hours, with a named person.
